QR payments and loyalty the merchant owns.

Dpay is an Australian payments fintech for small merchants. This walk-through shows what is built and running on 1 November 2026: the till app, the customer app, the merchant dashboard and the POS integration, all on a mock payment rail until the partner is signed.

1 · The problem

Card acceptance costs a small merchant 1 to 2%, and nobody can see it.

Surcharging was banned on 1 October 2026, so the merchant now carries the whole cost.

Terminal rent and the bank's bundled fee hide what each payment really costs.

Loyalty programs exist only for chains.

2 · What Dpay is

One sentence.

Dpay is QR payments account to account (PayID now, PayTo once the customer has an agreement) at a lower per-transaction cost than cards, with a loyalty program the merchant funds and owns, and one customer identity across every merchant that uses Dpay.

Dpay never holds funds: the payment partner settles straight to the merchant. Cards come in Phase 2 through a partner.

4 · The till app · charge

Type the amount, tap Charge · QR.

A dynamic PayID QR appears with a countdown.

The POS reference is shown big so the cashier keys it into the POS.

Card · tap is the Phase 2 button: present, disabled until the partner is signed.

5 · The till app · customer by phone

The cashier types the customer's mobile.

The sale earns points the moment it settles.

No app needed on the customer's side for this.

The same number later signs the customer into the app, with the points already there.

6 · The till app · Today and Cash-up

The day, by hour, then the close.

Today: payments by hour, how many were keyed into the POS, who is still awaiting a payer, exceptions.

Cash-up: the day's totals including refunds and late settlements. Exceptions block the close. Export for the accountant.

Refunds need a manager PIN.

7 · The customer app · Home

One card per merchant.

Balance, dollar value, a progress bar in the merchant's own brand colour, and "rewards ready" when there is something to use.

Sign-in is phone plus a 6-digit code, the same phone given at the till.

Points earned before the app was installed are already there.

8 · The customer app · redeem

Pick a reward, show the code.

The code is 8 characters, full screen, with a 15-minute countdown.

The cashier types it at the till. Points move only then, exactly once, never below zero, never past stock.

9 · The customer app · Activity

The statement.

Earned, redeemed, clawed back on refund.

Per merchant and per store, with the sale that caused each line.

10 · The merchant dashboard · Overview

The business at a glance.

Revenue with forecast pace, revenue by store, turnover, QR share, payment received by type, refunds.

Rolling 7 and 30-day periods or any date range. All stores or one.

11 · The merchant dashboard · Compare this offer

Paste the bank's quote. See the month it would have cost.

The merchant enters a bank's or terminal provider's rates: debit, credit, blended, eftpos, fixed cents, terminal rent.

The saving is computed from a benchmark snapshotted on each charge, never from today's rate, so history never changes.

12 · The merchant dashboard · Settings

Everything the owner controls, with a change log.

Business and brand colour. Card benchmark versions. Rewards editor: add, pause, resume, retire, stock.

Manager PIN. Staff invite and roles. Scoped API keys with an IP allow-list. Webhook endpoints.

Every configuration change is written to the change log with who did it.

14 · Security and compliance, as built

What is already enforced.

  • Money as integer cents, never floats.
  • Row-level security forced on every tenant and customer table, with tests that fail the build.
  • No card data ever touches Dpay. PCI DSS SAQ A pack written.
  • API keys scoped and audited. Staff and configuration audit trails.
  • JWT with HS256 or the Supabase JWKS. Secrets never in the repository.
